# Simulated student conversation

This is a simulated student trial, not testimony from a real student. The coordinator supplied the student replies. Questions and replies below are the actual exchange used to determine the app.

## Plan interview

Agent: Who will use this and what pricing decision will it support (one product / one period)? Confirm USD with price, variable unit cost, whole units sold, fixed cost for same period; should all monetary inputs allow only cents? For zero/negative unit contribution with positive fixed cost, propose “No break-even at this price”; with zero fixed cost, zero units already breaks even, while negative margin loses money on every sale. Agree? What misleading assumption should be emphasized (constant costs, all units sold, demand excluded), and any input ranges you want?

Simulated student: I am an MBA student assessing a small notebook venture for one sales period. I want to compare profit and the number of units needed to cover costs for one product. Yes: USD, cents only, whole units, all four inputs nonnegative; cap each at 1,000,000 for this teaching tool. Agree with your zero/negative contribution and zero-fixed-cost descriptions. Make clear the model assumes constant unit price/cost, sells every entered unit, and excludes taxes, capacity and demand response. Start with price $20, cost $12, quantity 100, fixed cost $500; my hand calculation is $300 profit, 62.5 theoretical break-even or 63 whole units. Please also explain contribution per unit and make units obvious. I agree to this scope; no accounts or live data.

Agent plan confirmation: Notebook venture, one period; four nonnegative inputs capped at 1,000,000, USD cents and integer quantity. Show revenue, variable/total cost, contribution per unit, profit, theoretical break-even (2 decimals, explanatory), and minimum whole units without loss. Calculate button, reset to provided example, field-specific errors hiding stale results; no data retention/network. Zero fixed cost means zero units already break even; negative margin warns each extra sale loses money. Positive fixed cost plus margin <= 0 means no break-even. Independent boundary examples: 12/12/100/500 gives -$500, no break-even; 10/12/100/0 gives -$200, 0 units only; .30/.20/3/.30 gives $0, 3 units. Desktop + 320px + keyboard checks required. Please confirm this plan and answer Evaluate question: where might this model mislead a notebook seller beyond the exclusions already supplied?

Simulated student: I confirm your summarized plan. My biggest worry is interpreting a lower break-even volume as evidence of a good price when raising the price can reduce demand, or forgetting unsold inventory/capacity. Please clearly distinguish entered quantity from forecast sales and say the app cannot determine an optimal price. Proceed.

## Evaluation review

Simulated student after browser evaluation: Assumptions are clear and acceptable; break-even is a cost-coverage threshold, not an optimal price.

## Requested second version

Simulated student: Add “Load decimal example” preset using 19.90 price / 19.80 cost / 1,000 quantity / 100 fixed so I can explore a cents-sensitive break-even case.

Agent: This adds a second synthetic preset. Calculation rules remain unchanged. Ten cents per unit × 1,000 units covers $100 fixed cost, so profit is $0.00 and theoretical/whole break-even is 1,000 units. The existing reset still restores the original scenario.
